## Compile the request

- Define the overall look, framing and depth anchors.
- Give each shot a distinct block, with one principal camera move and one subject action.
- Use concrete, feasible action beats. Shorter shots can be easier to direct.
- Put brief spoken lines in a dedicated dialogue block.
- Separate actual duration, resolution and input controls from prose.
- Use a supplied image to establish the opening only if the selected route supports that behavior.
- Never invent end-frame, seed, keyframe or negative-prompt fields.

Higgsfield's tutorial also recommends clear foreground, middle-ground and background anchors, separated dialogue and explicit shot structure [2].

## Original shot example

> Look: bright monochrome architectural study, soft daylight.\
> Framing: low wide view, white panel in the foreground, a black curved opening in the middle distance, pale studio wall behind.\
> Camera: one slow forward dolly.\
> Action: the foreground panel passes out of view while the opening grows larger. All structures remain stationary.\
> Audio: quiet studio ambience.

This describes a continuous shot. Do not add a multi-shot template unless the requested deliverable actually needs cuts.

## Diagnose before revising

The model improvises unwanted events: remove ambiguity and narrow the action. Too many beats: split into independently checked shots. Spatial discontinuity: strengthen reference imagery and screen-direction anchors. Dialogue truncation: reduce spoken words or separate speech production.

Identical prompts can yield different results. A rerun is a fresh sample, not a deterministic repair. Use the accepted output as an edit/reference when supported.
